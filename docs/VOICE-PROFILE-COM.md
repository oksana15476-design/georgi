# Профиль голоса praxenai.com

По скиллу `brand-voice` (схема — `.claude/skills/brand-voice/references/voice-profile-schema.md`). Собран 9 октября 2026 из текстов сайта, которые утвердил владелец. Копирайтер пишет по нему, главред сверяет. Если владелец пришлёт свои тексты (посты, письма клиентам), профиль пересобираем по ним.

```text
VOICE PROFILE
=============
Author: Praxen AI (praxenai.com), company voice for UK, US and EU businesses
Goal: blog articles, LinkedIn posts, emails and site copy that read like the site
Confidence: medium (site copy only; no posts or emails from the owner yet)

Source Set
- H1 and subheads of service pages, lib/intl.ts (services: ai-receptionist, ai-automation, ai-consultancy, ai-chatbot, ai-agents, ai-training)
- H1 and subheads of industry and department pages, lib/intl.ts
- short answers ("question" / "answer") on service pages, lib/intl.ts

Rhythm
- short declarative sentences, 8–20 words; a headline sentence, then one sentence of detail
- lists of real actions in one sentence ("read, checked and entered into your CRM")

Compression
- dense: what it does, where, how fast, what it costs; no warm-up paragraph

Capitalization
- conventional; sentence case in headings; product names as spelled (HubSpot, Xero, WhatsApp)

Parentheticals
- rare; used only to narrow a fact, never for jokes

Question Use
- only as real customer questions in FAQ and section titles ("What does an AI receptionist cost?"); no rhetorical or bait questions

Claim Style
- concrete mechanism + boundary: "Every action is logged", "Nothing is posted or paid without finance signing off"
- numbers with units and terms: "about two weeks", "from £665 setup + £174/month", "up to 12 people"
- prices exclude VAT and match lib/intl.ts; examples are labelled as examples

Preferred Moves
- name the task, the tool and the person who approves
- second person ("your calendar", "your team")
- British spelling (organise, programme), "for businesses"

Banned Moves
- brand-voice hard bans: fake curiosity hooks, "not X, just Y", "no fluff", LinkedIn thought-leader cadence, bait questions, "Excited to share", founder-journey filler, corny parentheticals
- humanizer patterns (§1–§26), first of all not-X-but-Y, one-line closers, forced triads, em dashes, AI words
- invented clients, reviews, statistics; "revolutionary", "game-changer"; mentioning Georgia in sales copy
- "for small businesses"

CTA Rules
- one CTA per piece, specific: "Book a free 30-minute audit", "See all prices"; never "learn more"

Channel Notes
- Blog: answer first, then the steps or the comparison the reader came for; 3 internal links with descriptive anchors
- LinkedIn: one claim per post, the article link at the end, 2–3 hashtags; no lesson-post cadence
- Email: subject says what is inside; one ask
```
