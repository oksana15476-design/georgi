# Legitimate Interests Assessment: B2B outreach emails (praxenai.com)

Kept on file and shown on request, as the privacy policy says (praxenai.com/privacy, section «Business contacts we email first»). Based on ICO guidance: legitimate interests and B2B marketing under UK GDPR and PECR.

## 1. Purpose test: what is the interest?

- Praxen AI sells AI receptionists, chatbots, automations and team training to UK businesses with roughly 5–50 staff.
- We want to tell a small number of relevant businesses about these services by email: one introduction and at most two follow-ups.
- Interest: promoting our services to businesses (direct marketing is recognised as a possible legitimate interest in UK GDPR Recital 47).
- Benefit to the recipient: a concrete offer that addresses a visible problem (missed calls, slow replies, manual admin), with a free audit and no obligation.

## 2. Necessity test: is this processing needed?

- Without a business contact we cannot reach the right companies; there is no less intrusive way to start a first conversation with a business that does not know us.
- Data is limited to what is needed: company name and number, a published business email (preferably a role address such as info@ or office@), a person’s name and job title only where the business publishes them for business contact, website, business phone, and short notes about the business relevant to the message.
- We do not buy lists, guess addresses, use personal email accounts (Gmail, Hotmail, etc.) or collect special category data.
- Volume is small and manual (about 10–15 emails a day), each written for the specific business.

## 3. Balancing test: do the individual’s interests override ours?

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Nature of data | Business contact details the business itself published. No sensitive data. |
| Reasonable expectations | A business that publishes a contact address for enquiries can reasonably expect occasional relevant B2B approaches. Named staff addresses are used only where published for business contact. |
| Who we contact | Limited companies, LLPs and other corporate bodies only (PECR «corporate subscribers»). Sole traders and partnerships are excluded unless they have given prior consent. |
| Impact | Low: up to three short emails, relevant to their business, no tracking pixels, no attachments. |
| Control | Every email identifies the sender, explains where the address came from, links to the privacy policy and offers a one-reply opt-out («no thanks»). Opt-outs are honoured immediately and permanently. |
| Safeguards | Companies House check that the company is active; skip any business whose site says it does not accept unsolicited sales emails; do-not-contact list checked before every batch. |

**Conclusion:** the interest is legitimate, the processing is necessary and limited, and with the safeguards above the individual’s interests do not override it. Lawful basis: UK GDPR Art. 6(1)(f). PECR reg. 22 does not require consent for emails to corporate subscribers; reg. 23 (sender identity, valid opt-out address) is met.

## 4. Operating rules

1. Sources: the business’s own website, Companies House, public business directories, public company or professional profiles. Record the source URL for each address.
2. Before each batch: check the do-not-contact list; check Companies House status (active Ltd/LLP/PLC).
3. Each email: sender name, Praxen AI, praxenai.com, hello@praxenai.com; where the address came from; privacy link; opt-out by replying «no thanks».
4. At most one introduction and two follow-ups per business, then stop.
5. Opt-out or any negative reply: stop at once; keep only email, company and date in the do-not-contact list.
6. No reply: delete the contact details within 12 months of the last email.
7. Requests to access, correct or delete data: answer within one month (privacy policy, «Your rights»).

## 5. Records

- Outreach sheet: company, number, email, source URL, date of each email, reply, outcome.
- Do-not-contact list: email, company, date of opt-out.
